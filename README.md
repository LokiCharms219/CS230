# CS230

Q: Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements. Who was the client? What type of software did they want you to design?
A: The client was The Gaming Room, and they requested a web-based version of their Android game Draw It or Lose It. The software needed to support multiple teams and players, ensuring that no game, team, or player instance was duplicated. Java was chosen for compatibility with the Android SDK, and singleton and iterator patterns were used to enforce uniqueness.

Q: What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?
A: I feel the documentation clearly outlined the use of design patterns to meet client requirements. The application of the singleton and iterator patterns was effectively explained, demonstrating thoughtful planning and prevention of object duplication.

Q: What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code?
A: Working through the design document helped me break down the system into manageable pieces and clarified how the classes would interact. This made it easier for me to translate the design into functional code.

Q: If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
A: I would revise the system architecture section with a visual diagram or more detailed component interactions. This would offer a clearer picture of data flow and dependencies.

Q: How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your software design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
A: My design was based on clear requirements from the client to support unique instances of games, teams, and players. Interpreting user needs was key in applying the appropriate design patterns. Considering user needs ensures that the software is functional, user-friendly, and aligned with business goals.

Q: How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a similar software application?
A: The approach emphasized object-oriented design and reusable code through inheritance and encapsulation. In future projects I intend to use UML diagrams, iterative development, and early feedback loops to strengthen alignment between design and implementation.
